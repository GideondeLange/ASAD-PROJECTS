1. Summary

The site has a strong technical foundation. Every page has its own title, description, single main heading and canonical address, nothing is broken inside the site, redirects are clean, and the structured data is more complete than most small-business sites. The weak spots are content accuracy (old prices still in the blog), page weight (large photos), social-sharing tags, and a few missing extras that help local search.

2. Prioritised fix list

High = affects what Google shows or what customers see; do first. Medium = clear gains. Low = tidy-ups.

High

1. Blog posts still show the old prices and old wording

Three blog posts say pods "start from R215,000" and that larger models cost "R285,000 to R430,000+". The 2026 brochure range runs from R45 000 (office pod) to R1 095 000 (3 Bedroom Pod). These old figures are also inside the hidden FAQ data that Google can show in search results, so Google may display the wrong price.

Affected: blog/best-uses-for-accommodation-pods, blog/tiny-home-on-stilts-tamboti-river-reserve, blog/why-invest-in-a-tiny-home. The posts also use "tiny home" wording that doesn't match the new Living Pods branding.

Fix: update the figures in both the visible text and the FAQ data to match the brochure, and link each post to the matching pod page.

High

2. Page titles too long or too short

Google shows about 60 characters of a title and cuts the rest off with "…". Measured: 14 of 25 titles are over 60 characters and 5 are over 70. The blog titles are the worst (up to 88 characters). Two titles are too short and waste the space: Gallery (26 characters) and Pods & Pricing (33).

Medium

3. Social sharing previews are thin

When a link is shared on Facebook, WhatsApp or LinkedIn, the preview comes from og: tags. All 25 pages have title, description, image and type, but none have og:url, og:site_name, og:locale or Twitter/X card tags. Some preview images are poor choices: the Contact page uses a 34 KB interior photo, and the Gallery page uses a 1 MB photo (slow to load for the preview). Preview images should be about 1200 × 630 pixels and under 300 KB.

Fix: add the missing tags to the shared page template and make one 1200 × 630 preview image per page type.

Medium

4. Photos are too heavy (this slows the site on phones)

The project holds 417 images, 119.5 MB in total. 51 are over 500 KB and 30 are over 1 MB. 37 are wider than 2000 pixels, which is more than any screen needs. Every image is JPEG; none use the smaller modern WebP or AVIF formats. On the live site, the home hero is 728 KB and the pod photos are 100 to 600 KB each. Heavy hero images are the main cause of a slow first screen, and Google uses speed as a ranking factor on mobile.

There are also duplicate originals in the project (for example chosen/asad-pod3.jpg and chosen/services-pod3.jpg are the same 2.3 MB photo), which only adds clutter.

Fix: resize the hero and card photos to the size they display, convert to WebP, and keep the first screen under about 150 KB. Visitors will notice the difference on mobile data.

Medium

5. No privacy policy or cookie notice

The site loads Google Analytics and the Meta (Facebook) Pixel, and the contact form collects names, phone numbers and locations. In South Africa this falls under POPIA. There is no privacy page, and none is listed in the sitemap or footer. Google and Meta ads policies also expect one.

Fix: add a short privacy page, link it in the footer, and mention the tracking tools and the contact form data.

Medium

6. Branded 404 page missing

A wrong address shows a plain Hostinger page titled "This Page Does Not Exist" with no navigation. It does correctly return a 404 status, which is good for Google, but visitors are left stranded.

Fix: add a simple branded 404.html with the menu and a link to the pods, and point the server at it.

Medium

7. Structured data gaps

See section 6 for the details: the business data has no opening closing time or map location, and pages like Contact, Services and Gallery have none.

Low

8. Sitemap dates are old

All 25 sitemap entries say "last updated 2026-10-30", even though several pages have changed since (and the page content really changed on 10 November). Google mostly ignores priority and changefreq, but it does use accurate lastmod dates to decide when to re-crawl.

Low

9. Gallery page has almost no text

The Gallery has 175 words and 77 images. Google reads images through the text around them. A short intro and a one-line caption per section would help it rank for searches like "steel pergola Gauteng" or "A-frame cabin".

Low

10. Small tidy-ups

  • Blog pages have no "skip to content" link (the other 20 pages do).
  • /asad-pods/ with a trailing slash serves the page instead of redirecting. The canonical tag prevents any harm, but a redirect is cleaner.
  • The web app manifest has a white background colour and no description, and its theme colour (#0D0D0D) differs from the page tag (#0b0b0b).
  • Leftover files in the project root (style.css, script.js, scraper.py, content-drafts) are not used by the site.
  • Google Fonts load from Google's servers. It works, but hosting the two fonts yourself is slightly faster and avoids sending visitor data to Google.

4. Technical files and how the site is served

robots.txt

Good. Allows all crawlers and points to the sitemap: Sitemap: https://asad.co.za/sitemap.xml. No page is blocked and none carries a noindex tag.

sitemap.xml

Good. 25 URLs, all with the clean https address, matching every page on the site. Valid XML. Weakness: all dates read 2026-10-30 (see fix 8). Submitting it in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools is the next step if it hasn't been done.

Speed settings from the server

  • HTTPS with Brotli compression and HTTP/3: good.
  • CSS, JavaScript and images are cached for 7 days: good. Longer (1 year) is safe for the CSS and JS because their file names change when the content changes.
  • CSS is 31 KB and JavaScript is 6 KB after building, which is small.
  • Web pages themselves are 20 to 22 KB: small. The photos are the whole speed story.

site.webmanifest, icons and favicon

Present and working: favicon (16/32/48 and .ico), Apple touch icon, and 192 and 512 pixel app icons. Minor tidy-ups only (see fix 10).

Head tags, in the order they load

Each page loads, in the head: character set, Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, viewport, icons, title, description, social tags, canonical, fonts, stylesheet, and the structured data. The character set comes first (fixed on 10 November), which removed the garbled price symbols. The tracking scripts load in the background and don't block the page.

How the site is built and published

  • The pages are built with Vite from HTML files, and published to Hostinger automatically whenever something is pushed to the main branch on GitHub (takes 1 to 2 minutes).
  • The contact form sends through your Afrihost mail server using PHPMailer. Passwords are stored as GitHub secrets and written at deploy time, never saved in the code: good practice.
  • Two-computer caution: the page generator in scripts/site is out of date compared with the other computer's pages. Running it here could overwrite that work. It should be brought back in line or retired.

7. Layout, structure and accessibility

  • Structure: every page uses a proper header, menu (nav), main area and footer. One H1 per page, with H2 sections underneath: clean.
  • Navigation: Pods, Services, Finishes, Gallery, Journal, "Get a Quote", plus the logo strip. Every page is reachable from the menu within one click, and every pod links to the next and previous pod.
  • Mobile: the viewport tag is on every page. I checked the home page, pods and gallery at phone width earlier: no sideways scrolling and the photos light up while scrolling.
  • Language: set to English on all pages. Because you serve South Africa only, a regional tag (en-ZA) would be slightly more precise.
  • Accessibility: skip-to-content link on 20 of 25 pages (missing on the blog), all photos have alt text. Text contrast on the dark hero relies on the overlay; the white text on the coloured photo remained readable in my checks.
  • Calls to action: "Enquire", "WhatsApp" and "Get a Quote" appear on every pod page and in the menu, and are now tracked by both Google Analytics and the Meta Pixel.

8. Security and privacy

  • Good: HTTPS everywhere, forced https and non-www, contact form has a hidden spam trap (honeypot) and checks every field on the server, mail passwords are not in the code, and .htaccess can't be read from outside.
  • Missing headers (medium): the server only sends upgrade-insecure-requests. Adding Strict-Transport-Security, X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options and Referrer-Policy is a few lines in .htaccess.
  • Exposed (low): /mail-config.php and the PHPMailer library files answer with a blank page rather than being blocked. They reveal no passwords, but a deny rule is cleaner.
  • Contact form (low): no rate limit. The honeypot stops most bots, but adding a simple limit per visitor would stop floods of fake enquiries.
  • Privacy: no privacy policy or cookie notice (see fix 5).

9. Local SEO and content opportunities

  • Business details are consistent across the site: 083 371 5807, info@asad.co.za, Unit 13, Turbo Park, Paul Smith Rd, Anderbolt, Boksburg, Reg No. 2018/589606/07. Please confirm the street spelling (the old site said "Paul Smit Road").
  • Google Business Profile: I can't see it from here. If it isn't set up and verified, that is the single biggest local-search win: it puts you on Google Maps for "steel pods near me".
  • Location pages: the site only mentions Boksburg and "nationwide". Pages such as "Pods in Johannesburg / Pretoria / Cape Town / KwaZulu-Natal" would capture searches from each area.
  • Search terms worth targeting: tiny homes South Africa, granny flat, backyard cottage, off-grid cabin, A-frame cabin, Airbnb pod, steel frame home, garden office. Each can be a pod page heading, a blog post or a section.
  • Blog: four posts is a start. One new post per month (for example "How much does a granny flat cost in 2026?" or "Do I need plan approval for a backyard pod?") would build search traffic over time.
  • Trust signals: the NHBRC logo is in the nav strip. Customer reviews or testimonials on the home page would add trust and allow star ratings later.
